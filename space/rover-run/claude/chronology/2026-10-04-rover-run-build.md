## Decisions

- **Loop = one line of the team sheet.** Per row: an optional stop (recharge or sample), then Go
  or Avoid, then the row's four cards flip. Two keys at most. (User asked for "choose 2 values,
  reveal, next square".) Spare turns became a pool of **stops**.
- **Rules retuned by simulator.** The rulebook's 8 battery / N+2 turns cannot carry a 14-row deck
  with 4 hazards (intended play needs ~15 battery). Swept battery × stops × recharge; kept
  **battery 8, recharge +2, Avoid −2, 6 stops**. All rulebook targets pass (`node tools/sim.mjs`):
  intended 18 (finishes, 1 sample), avoid-any-warning 12 (battery death row 12), slope-only dies in
  the row-5 storm, random 4.9, hindsight ceiling 19 (false alarms are meant to cost a little).
- **Slope at map scale.** First slope buckets (20 m roughness) said the rim rows were steep but the
  map didn't show it. Re-measured at 200 m smoothing — the scale the contour spacing shows: rows
  10–12 steep (11–12°), floor flat. Cards now match what the eye reads on the map.
- **Dot-matrix screen, not xterm.** Canvas display lists of dots (3×6 sub-grid per cell) + glyphs,
  so death effects can move the same dots as particles. Contours at 35 m, film palette
  (blue-grey low → rust high), white route, hollow sample circles as in the film.
- **Deaths:** storm = dune front from top-right turning dots to sand; battery = CRT collapse to a
  line, then a point; sand = everything spirals into the rover. Then a scanline rebuild and a
  film-style replay of the run (route drawn on, samples circled, hazards marked, battery curve).
- **Verification via new sols.** Ghost cards remember every reveal across retries (as on a table),
  which allows memorising. After a finish, `N` = same route, new hidden ground and weather, same
  patterns. Four extra sols generated by `tools/gen.mjs`; each passes every target.
- **Honesty:** footer "real terrain + route · simulated hazards". Haze on sol 1 sits where tau ran
  highest (rows 4, 7); storms, ground, sand and the later sols are authored.

## Blind playtest (Cursor agent, GPT-5.6 Sol, 6 runs) — `benchmarks/v1-blind-playtest-gpt56.md`

Scores 4, 7, 6, 7, 17, **19**. It beat the intended 18 by **memorising** sol 1 through the ghost
cards. It never stated soft→sand or haze→storm, and called the first deaths "forced, no clear
advance warning". The map was used for route following, not slope reasoning. Repeating the solved
first half was "mechanical". Fun: persistent knowledge, and planning the last run exactly.

Lesson: same-deck retries plus ghost cards reward memory, not a model. New sols must be the default,
not a reward, and the history must show *transitions* (row → next row), not positions.
That feeds v2.
