## 3. Make the diagram carry the concept

The outbound view shows many orange alternatives diverging from Earth. Mission Control selects an intercept at the Moon's future position, labelled three days later, rather than its current position. The Moon moves around Earth throughout the diagram. A surface mark follows the Earth-facing basis to represent synchronous rotation; it does not spin independently at an arbitrary speed.

After arrival, communication disappears when the Moon blocks the Earth link. A bounded Moon-relative retrograde burn uses narrow guidance geometry while the Earth link is occluded. The authored local radius contracts from 0.9 to 0.76 diagram units. The spacecraft completes four revolutions around the moving Moon, leaving a trace in the common world frame: successive loops form a chain instead of repeatedly drawing the same stationary ellipse. The sequence finishes with the orbital diagram and a fade.

Implemented timing: 0–12 seconds Mission Control and future-target transit; 12–16 seconds bounded local burn; 16–30 seconds repeated lunar revolutions, including the 29–30 second fade.

Distances and both forms of orbital motion are accelerated nonuniformly for legibility. “Three days” is the planned intercept interval represented by the diagram, not a real-time or numerically integrated trajectory. The film is a schematic concept, not an orbital simulation or historical mission reconstruction. The optional narration is separate; v2 is silent.

Visual review identified a misleading early intercept: the spacecraft appeared adjacent to “Moon now” while the future continuation was too faint. The selected path was moved below the current Moon and its solid continuation brightened toward the future target. The final reviewed still separates the current Moon from the selected route; the fan, narrow local geometry and moving loop chain are visible. This is a concept review, not user acceptance.

Checkpoint: storyboard, narration, reviewed diagram and master export completed.
