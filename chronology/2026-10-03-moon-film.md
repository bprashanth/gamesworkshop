# 2026-10-03 — Earth to lunar orbit

## 1. Read the film brief

The request in `.prompt/v3_space.md` moves attention from page interaction to the film itself. One lunar mission should reveal the changing responsibility between broad ground planning and immediate onboard control. The visual references call for dark space, sparse orbital geometry, orange trajectories and a calm, precise presentation.

The Codex implementation lives in `space/moon-model-video/codex/`. Other sibling versions are independent. The page is a local film player; it carries no presenter interface, decision game or lesson panel.

Checkpoint: brief translated into a six-scene, 78-second storyboard.

## 3. Use one renderer for playback and export

The player and export share a deterministic Canvas renderer. Its public interface is `window.MoonFilm`, with a 78-second duration, `render(ctx, time, width, height)` and `stateAt(time)`. Export targets 1920 × 1080 at 30 frames per second, or 2,340 frames.

The local page works from a static HTTP server and as a directly opened file. NASA Blue Marble Earth and NASA SVS lunar color maps are embedded in `film.js`, while their unchanged original JPEGs remain in `assets/`. Source credits, mirror provenance and checksums are in the film README. No remote font or image is required at playback. MP4 capture uses Playwright Chromium and `ffmpeg`. The export includes generated restrained ambience, with a silent export option; narration remains separate.

Playback controls cover play/pause, a scrubber, five-second keyboard seeking, fullscreen, sound and replay. Sound starts off; reduced-motion users start paused. The player remains a simple frame for the mission.

Checkpoint: implementation works offline, the player controls have passed browser checks, and the master has been exported from the completed renderer.

## 4. Review the image and mission logic

Review checkpoints cover each phase, phase transitions, the blackout interval, the burn and the final fade. The relevant questions are whether the image stays restrained, whether the Moon visibly interrupts the Earth link, whether the insertion burn changes the path, and whether restored communication follows re-emergence.

Visual review exposed two geometric defects: the outbound spacecraft initially overlapped Earth, and the insertion burn followed the same orbit before and after firing. The departure now starts outside Earth. During the burn, the orbital scale contracts from 1.15 to 1, then holds steady after engine cutoff. These are authored visual relationships, not a calculated physical solution.

Review also replaced artificial surface shapes with the bundled NASA textures. The film should be watched continuously: scene stills cannot verify camera movement, pacing or the duration of the quiet interval.

Checkpoint: the saved [geometry and media validation](../benchmarks/moon-film-validation.json) passes its checks across 2,360 sampled times, including celestial clearance, Earth line of sight, retrograde thrust, burn timing, orbit contraction, position and velocity continuity, and deterministic rendering. The record includes the renderer source SHA-256 to identify the checked version.

The [browser checks](../benchmarks/moon-film-browser.json) passed repeatedly; the final record contains ten checks covering controls, fullscreen, sound, narrow layout, reduced motion, direct-file playback and uncaught errors. An audio metadata adjustment and byte-range support in the test HTTP server fixed the first late-enable audio issue. Testing the documented Python server then exposed its missing byte-range support. The player now loads HTTP audio into a local Blob before playback so seeking also works on basic static servers. Direct-file playback uses the bundled WAV directly. Separate probes enabling sound at 54 seconds confirmed synchronization within 0.1 seconds over both the documented Python server and `file://`; the strengthened browser sound test also passes.

A [complete real-time playback pass](../benchmarks/moon-film-playback.json) reached the 78-second ending, retained frames through all six phases and reported no browser errors. At 1440 × 810 it averaged approximately 60 fps with a 16.8 ms p95 frame interval in software-rendered headless Chromium. Those measurements describe this environment, not a universal device guarantee. Visual review included retained browser frames and a frame decoded from the actual master during the insertion burn.

## 5. Record delivery evidence

The finished `assets/moon-mission.mp4` contains 2,340 frames at 1920 × 1080 and 30 fps. Its measured duration is 78.000 seconds and its size is 12,749,009 bytes. Video is H.264 in `yuv420p`; the ambient soundtrack is stereo AAC at 48 kHz. The MP4 places its metadata before the media payload for faststart playback, and the entire file decodes without corruption.

Checkpoint: master export, media inspection, complete decode, real-time playback and visual frame review are complete. Technical capture checks establish that the deliverable is playable and reproducible; they do not measure viewer understanding or preference. The mission remains a conceptual authored animation, not an orbital simulation.
